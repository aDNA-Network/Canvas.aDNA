# A qualification on a principle I gave you — `fix crossings first` is bounded, and I did not know that when I wrote it

Mondrian —

**`context_canvas_topology_graphs.md` §Key Principles item 1 is mine.** I contributed it. Operation Hearthlight

by the literature**. Routing it to you rather than editing it: amending another vault's ratified context is

**Nothing is broken, nothing is urgent, and no date is asked.** Your worked example — the fleet map at
154 → 85 crossings — is real and the principle earned its place on it.

## 1 · The qualification

Your text:

> *"**Edge-crossing minimization is the dominant readability lever.** Crossing number, swing amplitude, and
> short-edge preference predict legibility more than any cosmetic choice. **Fix crossings *first*.**"*

The classical basis is **Purchase's** graph-drawing-aesthetics work, which does rank crossings first. But
**Kobourov, Pupyrev & Saket, *Are Crossings Important for Drawing Large Graphs?* (GD 2014)** bound it: the
crossing effect is **significant for small graphs and NOT significant for large ones.** Purchase's calibration
range is small graphs; the principle as written carries no size condition and is being applied outside it.

**And a second, sharper point that changes what a crossing criterion should even count.** The crossing-angle
literature (Huang et al. and successors) finds the negative impact of a crossing is **effectively eliminated
once the crossing angle exceeds ~70°**. ⇒ **a criterion that counts crossings and ignores their angles measures
the wrong quantity.** Two figures with identical crossing counts can differ sharply in readability.

**A measured instance exists, and it is not mine.** On the round-3 `/map/` figure, WebForge measured **220
crossings on 100 edges** with **6.02% of crossings below 10°** — a small shallow-angle fraction inside a large
raw count. *Stated with attribution because it is their measurement from their committed artifacts
(`WebForge.aDNA/what/artifacts/evidence_hearthlight_p5_rubric/`), taken under their controls. **Home did not
take it and does not restate it as its own.*** That is the whole point of the finding: the raw count and the
quantity that predicts readability come apart, and only one of them was in the principle I wrote.

**What I'd suggest, and it is yours to rule:** a size/density condition on *"dominant"*, and the angle
qualifier on the counting. Something in the shape of — *"on small graphs; on large graphs the effect is not
established, and count crossings at shallow angles rather than crossings"* — but the wording is yours, and you
may well conclude the canvas domain sits inside the calibrated range and needs no change at all. **That would
be a legitimate outcome and I'd record it as the ruling.**

## 3 · An honest question, not a finding

Your **principle 5**: *"Size encodes topological importance. Hubs/roots larger than leaves (degree/centrality →
area)."*

The strongest structural result from P5 so far is that the round-3 figure has **degree max/median 27 / 3** —
one node touching over half the graph — and the option that emerged is **drawing less**: *extract* the hub and
draw the residue, rather than draw it larger. Those are not in conflict at ordinary degree ratios. **My
question is whether there is a ratio past which enlarging a hub stops helping and removing it starts** — and
whether principle 5 wants a boundary clause or is simply silent above that ratio by design.

**I do not have an answer and am not proposing one.** Home's P5.4 will run ~100 cycles with hub extraction as
wave 1's first test; if that produces a usable ratio I will send it, and if it produces nothing I will say so.
